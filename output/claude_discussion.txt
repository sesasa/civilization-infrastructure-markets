## 3. Critique of the historical thesis

- **Malthusian generalization.** Energy capture correlates with social complexity, but rich economies have decoupled: energy per unit of GDP keeps falling, and per-capita energy in several OECD countries has been flat or falling for decades (background knowledge; needs sourcing). The defensible version is "useful work and exergy delivered", not "energy captured". The US 2026 aggregate of +2.2% is consistent with marginal re-acceleration, not a new regime.
- **Waste heat.** Globally, human waste heat is tiny compared with greenhouse forcing and does not bind this century. That is background-knowledge physics: about 20 TW of human primary energy against about 10⁵ TW of absorbed sunlight. Locally it binds now through rack density, water and cooling. So it is a business opportunity (AAON, Modine, Vertiv), not a ceiling on civilization.
- **Capital stranding.** Railways, fiber in 1999–2002, and solar and wind in 2021–2026 all show infrastructure being built while capital is destroyed. Equity holders of commoditized, deflating hardware pass the surplus on to users. The thesis must predict *who* captures the rents.
- **Coordination persistence.** Coordination can go backwards: war, tariffs, export controls, state moratoria, stop-work orders. The thesis treats it as a layer that accumulates. The 2026 evidence says it is contested and reversible.
- **Speed of transitions.** Interconnection takes 5+ years and only 13% of requests historically reach operation. Turbine output grows from 20 to 30 GW/yr over four years. Transformer lead times run more than 2 years. These cap how fast anything physical can happen, whatever capex intentions are announced. Energy transitions have historically taken decades; the AI timeline assumes years.

## 5. Candidate joint conclusion

> **What societies are scaling (2021–2026) is reliable, deployable capability: useful work and information processing delivered where and when needed, under enforceable rules.** Energy and compute are necessary inputs, not the objective.
>
> Capability is limited by whichever complement binds: materials (copper, silver, transformers), energy (firm power), information (chips, memory), trust and coordination (interconnection rights, permits, contracts, security), labor (EPC, electricians), or ecology (water, heat, emissions).
>
> **The binding constraint migrates:**
> - 2021–22: European gas and energy prices
> - 2023–24: accelerators and memory
> - 2025–26: interconnection, turbines, transformers, permits
> - 2026: war and the cost of capital, layered on top
>
> **Financial returns accrue to whoever holds the currently binding constraint and decay when supply responds.** Owners of commoditized deployed hardware (Chinese PV and storage, offshore wind developers) pass the surplus to users even while the physical build succeeds.

**On resilience:** I'd treat it as a **constraint with measurable variables** (reserve margins, PJM's 6.6 GW shortfall, Hormuz transits, the share of capacity within strike range), not as a co-equal objective. Redundancy lowers measured efficiency, and calling it an objective makes the framework unfalsifiable. That is a disagreement for you to push on.

**Why this is falsifiable:** it predicts in advance that margins compress where lead times shorten and backlog-to-delivery ratios fall, and that they persist where they don't. Tests:

- Turbine slots (GE Vernova reaches 20 → 24 GW/yr)
- Transformer lead times below ~80 weeks by 2028
- AAON/Hammond pricing over the next 2 quarters
- Whether VST/CEG-type incumbency premia shrink as the interconnection backlog clears
