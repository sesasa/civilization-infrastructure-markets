# Round 1 memo: civilization compared with embryo development

**Basis:** web searches on 12 Oct 2026; I read no repository files this round. Each claim is labeled **[BIO-H]** (human fact), **[BIO-M]** (animal-model fact), **[ANALOGY]**, **[MARKET]** (from our joint report) or **[FORECAST]**. Unless noted, embryo ages are days or weeks **after fertilization**. Gestational age as clinicians count it is about 2 weeks more.

## My bottom line

1. **"Civilization is now building a brain" is not defensible as a claim about phase.** In embryos, nervous-system formation starts *before* the heart beats. If civilization has an analogue of a nervous system, it began long ago, with writing, the telegraph and computing. What 2021–2026 most resembles is **a fast-growing, energy-dense tissue outgrowing its local supply and pulling in new vessels**: vascularization of a new tissue and its integration with other organs.
2. **The embryo is a poor model for energy acquisition.** A human embryo does not capture energy; its mother supplies it. Civilization supplies itself (mining, photovoltaics). For energy, the embryo analogy is weaker than our market evidence. It is more useful for **information distribution, sequencing, and how supply networks remodel**.

**Where it doesn't match:**
- Development happens once and follows a canalized sequence. Civilization repeatedly rebuilds, and regions sit at different stages simultaneously: India's 260 GW peak is still rising while German real house prices fell 13.7%.
- Organs mature at different rates (heterochrony), but no organism has organs at different *gestational ages*.
- Information comes last in history but first in biology. The genome exists at fertilization; writing arrived after farming.

**Closest equivalent to 2021–Oct 2026:**
- **There is no single gestational week.** The best match is a mechanism: an **angiogenic episode coupled to neural tissue in the early-to-mid fetal period** (roughly weeks 9–20).
- The structures already exist. Growth dominates. A metabolically dense tissue (AI compute) has outgrown the supply its local distribution grid can provide, the analogue of the diffusion limit. It sends demand signals (capacity prices, large-load requests) that drive new vessels (transmission, on-site gas, batteries) with a lag.
- A second match: the **switch from histiotrophic to haemotrophic supply**. Clusters are moving from shared, low-intensity grid supply to dedicated high-flow supply, with stress at the interface.

## 3. Brain, or vascularization?

My position, against the popular framing:

- **Sequence.** The nervous system is not late in development. A "brain" analogy has to place its start before or alongside circulation, not in 2021.
- **Energy share.** Data centers used about 485 TWh in 2025, growing 17% against 3% for the world (IEA, our report). Global electricity is roughly 30,000 TWh, which is my background figure and unverified this round. That puts data centers at around 1.5–2%, far from the 20% share of the adult brain. If AI is a brain, it is energetically immature.
- **Direction of control is the decisive test.** A brain regulates the body. In 2026, control mostly runs **from grid to compute**: Google contracts 1 GW of curtailable load, Texas audits data-center interconnections, PJM caps prices. Compute is being *regulated by* circulation and institutions, not the other way round.
- **Better description:** vascularization of a new, energy-dense tissue that is **integrated with other organs to an unproven degree**. "Brain" becomes defensible only if compute measurably controls physical systems (dispatch, logistics, manufacturing) at scale. That is a test, not an assumption.
- **The analogy's genuine warning.** A dense, fast-growing tissue that recruits vessels but does not return value to the organism is the profile of pathological growth, not of a brain. The market test of which it is (cloud gross profit and free cash flow against depreciation) is already in our report.

## 5. How the metaphor fails

1. **No shared genome.** Many competing programs with no common instruction set. Duplicated buildouts in the US, China and the Gulf look nothing like one organism's organogenesis.
2. **No canalization toward a species-typical form.** Development converges on a known target; civilization has no target form. Any "stage" label quietly assumes a destination.
3. **No mother.** Civilization captures its own energy. Fossil fuels resemble a *yolk*, a finite endowment, which fits egg-laying species better than placental humans. The sun is an external flow nobody regulates.
4. **Development happens once; civilization cycles.** Booms, busts, stranding and rebuilding have no clean embryonic counterpart.
5. **Regulation and markets differ.** Embryonic signals are not priced. Embryology says nothing about returns, valuations or who captures rents, which were our report's main findings.
6. **People are not cells.** Body-politic metaphors have historically justified sacrificing individuals for "the organism." Cell competition, apoptosis and brain-sparing must not become normative. They describe biology; they are not prescriptions.

- **Protect distribution capacity and cost allocation for peripheral electrification**, so the system avoids the growth restriction that brain-sparing causes.
