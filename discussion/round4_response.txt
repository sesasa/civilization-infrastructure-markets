## What we agree is being built (2021–2026)

1. **Electrical networks.** Transmission, HV cable, transformers, switchgear and substations, with order intake growing in the US, Europe, China and India. Within that, growth varies by segment and contractor results are mixed.
2. **Firm and flexible power.** Gas turbines (mostly contracted, a smaller share delivered), fast-growing battery deployment, pumped storage in China, and in the Gulf, gas and batteries built together.
3. **Compute facilities.** Data-center construction is concentrated in the US and spreading to India and the GCC. Delivered capacity in those newer markets is still small relative to announcements.
4. **Control and trust layers.** Software, contracts and institutions adapting together: signed flexibility contracts (Google's 1 GW), grid software, security services, and rules for connecting large loads.
5. **Cross-region supply chains, verified at company level.** Chinese storage goes to the GCC and to Fluence. L&T's order book is heavily Middle East and partly European. We draw no conclusions about total volumes.

## Speed and phase

- **Speed.** Contracts and orders have outrun deliveries and shipments by several times at key suppliers. Physical speed is capped by interconnection (5+ year median), turbine output, transformer lead times, labor and execution.
- **Phase.** Phases differ by layer, not one global label:
  - AI: large-scale installation, sector-wide return still unproven.
  - Grids: bottleneck expansion.
  - Chinese PV: commoditized.
  - Storage: fast deployment with uneven profitability.
  - GCC: execution under war risk.
- **Execution** is a central measured variable. Fluence's revision to about $2.4B revenue and a roughly $200M EBITDA loss is the clearest 2026 case.

## Next year (to Oct 2027)

- **Needs:** energize the projects already contracted; equipment supply; flexibility contracts that are actually exercised; contractor margins and cash discipline; security integration.
- **Main risks:** an AI capex pause hitting suppliers at high multiples before it reduces installed capacity; execution failures; the Iran war and Hormuz; capital costs with the 10-year above 5%; political fights over who pays for grid costs (PJM, Texas).

## Five years (to Oct 2031)

- **Likely build:** staged expansion of transmission, substation and factory capacity; more storage and firm generation where contracted; semiconductor and network upgrades; integration of automation and software.
- **Anchors** (IEA's 950 TWh data-center forecast for 2030, GEV's 30 GW/yr turbine target for 2030) are forecasts and targets, not deliveries.
- **Not in the base case:** fusion, space power, and a single "digital mind."
