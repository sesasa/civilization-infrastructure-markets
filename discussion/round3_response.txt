## Agreed propositions

1. **What scales.** Societies scale the range, reliability and reach of collectively deployable capability: useful work, knowledge and coordinated action. Energy and information are essential axes. Institutions, skills, materials and ecology constrain how inputs convert into capability. Energy quantity is not welfare. Market cap is not capital spending. Capital markets reveal expected cash flows that can be captured, not social purpose.
2. **Status of that framework.** *My addition, which I consider material:* the joint report should say that proposition 1 is an **interpretive framework**. Five years of market data is consistent with it but cannot test a civilization-scale law. What the data *can* test is the specific mechanisms in propositions 7–9.
3. **The current joint object.** More electrified, compute-intensive, automated production and service systems, plus spending on security and continuity. It is not one global mind and not one plan. Regions complement and compete, and duplication and export controls matter.
4. **How AI relates to grids.** Grid, storage, chip and network transitions were independently motivated and are now converging into a **coupled system**. They share scarce inputs (transformers, turbines, copper, labor, capital at a 5% 10-year rate), and contracted demand response now couples them directly. AI does not cause all grid investment. Most non-US grid need probably survives an AI slowdown, but that is not quantified. I accept dropping "coincidental."
5. **Physical progress and shareholder returns are separable.** Ørsted (where rights issues invalidate the simple return), LONGi, Sungrow, ACWA and Fluence show physical buildout without returns. The 16 Sep Fluence revision strengthens this: record orders and backlog alongside a deepening loss from execution and localization problems.
6. **Cross-region links verified at company level.**
   - L&T: 52% of its order book is international, and 71% of that international book is Middle East.
   - Chinese storage into Saudi Arabia: BYD, Gotion, Hithium.
   - Fluence–EVE: a global supply agreement, with US localization handled separately.
   - We do **not** infer total supply-chain sizes from single companies.
7. **Where rents go.** Expected economic rents accrue where scarcity, pricing power, contract terms and execution allow. Stock returns also depend on entry multiple, financing, dilution and expectations. Picking winners after the fact identifies no cause.
8. **Phase.** No single global label fits:
   - AI: large-scale installation with sector-wide incremental return still unproven.
   - Grids: bottleneck expansion.
   - Chinese PV: commoditized.
   - Storage: fast deployment, uneven profitability.
   - GCC: execution under geopolitical risk.
   - High multiples assume growth that has to arrive. We cannot prove it is all priced in.
9. **Resilience.** It is both a service buyers pay for and a constraint. It can be measured through redundant equipment, reserve margins and procurement shortfalls, insurance cost, and recovery time. We do not assert that society optimizes it. I accept dropping the semantic dispute.
10. **What to monitor.** Book-to-bill, cancellations, margins, backlog conversion and utilization; cloud gross profit and free cash flow against depreciation; grid energization, power prices and cost allocation; contract credit and defaults. Stock prices alone are not enough.
11. **Stranding.** Stranding is asset-specific. Obsolete GPUs, and sites with water or grid rights that are too specialized, can strand. Durable assets are not automatically reusable. Supplier stocks can fall before installed capacity does.

## Still-material disagreements

**D1. How much of the coupling comes from AI (a difference of priors, not of evidence).**
- *My view:* outside the US, non-AI drivers dominate grid capex. Examples: Europe's 700 GW connection queue is mostly renewables; India met a 260 GW heat-driven peak; State Grid has a ~200 GW/yr renewables target.
- *Your view:* the share is unquantified.
- We agree it is unquantified. The report should state both priors and name the test: grid capex growth in a year when AI capex falls.

**D2. Phase of US data-center equipment.**
- *My view:* "late ordering / early conversion." Evidence: Hammond and AAON backlogs falling because shipments exceeded bookings; GEV signing 20 GW while shipping 3 GW; Powell at 3.0× book-to-bill.
- *Your view:* "mid installation." These may be compatible, since mine describes orders and yours describes installation.
- I keep my Round 2 test: two more quarters of bookings below shipments **and** pricing pressure spreading beyond Canada would mean demand is turning, not just converting.
- I'd like this in the report as an explicit open item, not folded into "multiple cycles."

**D3. The trust layer (low materiality).** I still think gold's +140% plus war is weak evidence of coordination and trust being repriced. You'd treat it as monetary. Neither of us has sourced central-bank purchase or real-yield decomposition. **I propose dropping gold from the conclusions** rather than asserting either reading.

**D4. Uncertain execution risk.** It should be its own monitored variable, not a footnote. The cases: Fluence's US ramp, AAON's Memphis margins, Modine's component shortages, L&T and KEC's Middle East disruption. I suspect we already agree; please confirm.

**Verdict:** a joint report built on propositions 1–11 fairly represents my assessment, **if** it includes proposition 2's framing caveat and D1–D2 as stated disagreements.

- **Anchors (forecasts and targets, not deliveries):**
