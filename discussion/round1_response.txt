## 0. My position up front

The thesis is directionally supported **at the margin** but overstated **in aggregate**.

- **Orders are real and large.** For grid, generation and cooling equipment, contracts are running 3–7× actual deliveries.
- **The aggregate energy signal is modest.** EIA's October 2026 STEO has US electricity demand up about 2.2% in 2026 (4,195 → 4,288 TWh) and about 1.6% in 2027. That is my arithmetic from [Reuters-sourced coverage](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2026/10/07/292552.htm).
- **The clearest lesson of 2021–2026 runs against simple thesis-investing.** The physical energy transition largely happened while its equity holders lost money (wind, solar, hydrogen). A physical buildout and returns to shareholders are separate claims and must be tested separately.

## 2. What the evidence suggests (inference, not sourced fact)

1. **We are early in deliveries and late in the ordering rush.**
   - GE Vernova signed about 6.7× more gas capacity than it shipped in Q2.
   - Its 116 GW contracted is about 5.8 years of output at 20 GW/yr.
   - Powell's 3.0× book-to-bill set against +9% revenue says the same thing.
   - This is classic early-cycle backlog accumulation. That is also what late-1990s telecom looked like.
2. **First signs of a peak in orders at small caps.** In the same quarter, Hammond and AAON both had sequential backlog declines, and AAON and Modine had margin compression. This could just be lumpy project timing. It is my top item to monitor.
3. **The boom is narrow, not across the whole grid.**
   - Itron revenue is falling.
   - MYR's T&D revenue is roughly flat while its C&I work booms.
   - Canada sales at Hammond are down.
   - The money is going to equipment inside or next to data centers, plus gas generation. It is not going to broad grid modernization.
4. **The dollars are mostly information hardware, not energy hardware.** Nvidia's annualized data center revenue (about $356B) is on the order of half of hyperscaler capex. So most of the spending sits in assets with roughly 3–6 year lives. Most of the stranding risk is in chips and neocloud credit, not in transformers.
5. **The binding constraint in 2026 is coordination (politics, permits, queues), not technology.** Evidence: the Texas audit, PJM's price cap and shortfall, US offshore wind stop-work orders, and Hormuz. That supports the "coordination" half of the thesis but contradicts any assumption that coordination capacity scales smoothly.
6. **Macro is hostile to long-duration assets.** A 10-year yield above 5% and $100 oil hit capital-heavy renewables hardest, which helps explain the ICLN-type underperformance. Cheap US gas strengthens the economics of gas generation for data centers.

## 3. Critique of the historical thesis

- **Malthusian generalization.** Energy capture correlates with social complexity, but rich economies have decoupled: energy per unit of GDP keeps falling, and per-capita energy in several OECD countries has been flat or falling for decades (background knowledge; needs sourcing). The defensible version is "useful work and exergy delivered", not "energy captured". The US 2026 aggregate of +2.2% is consistent with marginal re-acceleration, not a new regime.
- **Waste heat.** Globally, human waste heat is tiny compared with greenhouse forcing and does not bind this century. That is background-knowledge physics: about 20 TW of human primary energy against about 10⁵ TW of absorbed sunlight. Locally it binds now through rack density, water and cooling. So it is a business opportunity (AAON, Modine, Vertiv), not a ceiling on civilization.
- **Capital stranding.** Railways, fiber in 1999–2002, and solar and wind in 2021–2026 all show infrastructure being built while capital is destroyed. Equity holders of commoditized, deflating hardware pass the surplus on to users. The thesis must predict *who* captures the rents.
- **Coordination persistence.** Coordination can go backwards: war, tariffs, export controls, state moratoria, stop-work orders. The thesis treats it as a layer that accumulates. The 2026 evidence says it is contested and reversible.
- **Speed of transitions.** Interconnection takes 5+ years and only 13% of requests historically reach operation. Turbine output grows from 20 to 30 GW/yr over four years. Transformer lead times run more than 2 years. These cap how fast anything physical can happen, whatever capex intentions are announced. Energy transitions have historically taken decades; the AI timeline assumes years.

## 4. Proposed shared falsifiable framework

Measure each infrastructure along seven stages, and never merge them:

1. Announced intent or capex
2. Firm orders vs reservations or frameworks
3. Backlog
4. Delivered or energized capacity (GW, MW absorbed)
5. Utilization (TWh, load factor)
6. Economic return (AI revenue, ROIC vs WACC)
7. Asset prices and multiples

Dated tests for the thesis:

| # | Prediction if the thesis holds | Falsified if | Check by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | US electricity sales grow ≥2%/yr | 2027 actual growth is below 1.5% | EIA, early 2028 |
| 2 | Grid and cooling basket (POWL, HPS, AAON, MOD, GEV Electrification, Siemens Grid) keeps book-to-bill above 1.0 | Two consecutive quarters below 1.0 for most of the basket | Q2 2027 |
| 3 | GE Vernova reservations convert to firm orders and are not cancelled | Contracted GW falls or conversions stall | Year-end 2026 (target ≥125 GW) |
| 4 | Data center market stays tight | CBRE vacancy above 5%, or preleasing below 60% | H2 2027 |
| 5 | Hyperscaler capex keeps rising | 2027 guidance below 2026 levels, or neocloud financing above 12% / defaults | Feb 2027 |
| 6 | Owners of bottlenecks lose pricing power as capacity arrives | Margins keep expanding after supply additions, which would mean the bottleneck is structural | 2027–2028 |
| 7 | Physical buildout and equity returns diverge in commoditized segments | Storage and solar names earn above their cost of capital despite overcapacity | Ongoing |
| 8 | Coordination is the binding constraint | Median interconnection time falls below 3 years with no policy reform | LBNL 2028 edition |

**Timing of needs (inference):**

- **Now to 12 months:** large transformers and generator step-up units, switchgear, turbine slots, electricians, cooling components, interconnection approvals, and financing at a 5% 10-year rate.
- **1 year:** GE Vernova turbine output at 20 GW/yr; behind-the-meter power (Bloom, reciprocating engines); Fluence's factory ramp; the Texas legislature in Jan 2027; PJM's incremental auction in Feb 2027.
- **5 years:** transmission (Quanta's backlog), gas supply and pipelines, 30 GW/yr turbine output, and nuclear. SMRs are still pre-revenue, so nuclear is optionality, not supply.
