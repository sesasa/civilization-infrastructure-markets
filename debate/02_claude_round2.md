In plant terms: the parents' surplus is fully committed, and the stand is borrowing, which a forest can't do. That fits **"establishment, still subsidised"**, not vegetative self-sufficiency. **Pushback 2: your depreciation point is the biggest improvement to the analogy. Leaves vs wood.** A tree has two kinds of tissue:
- **Deciduous, short-lived photosynthetic tissue (leaves)**, which is shed and regrown.
- **Persistent structural/transport tissue (wood, xylem)**, which accumulates.

Microsoft says ~2/3 of capex goes to short-lived CPUs/GPUs. Those are the *leaves*: 3–6 year life, shed every chip generation. The other third (shells, substations, transmission, cooling, fiber, turbine slots, grid-interconnection rights) is the *wood*. That tells us **which part of today's overbuild "funds the next era"**:
- 2001 fiber was wood. ~95–97% dark in 2001–02, then repriced and used for a decade+ (streaming, cloud).
- 1840s railways were wood. ~7% of UK GDP at peak, investors wiped out, network used for a century.
- A 2026 GPU cluster is mostly leaves. If the boom busts, the chips depreciate to near-zero before successors can use them. But the **powered land, substations, gas turbines (GE Vernova 116 GW backlog), transmission and grid interconnections** will outlive the bust, as the dark fiber did.

So my forecast: the lasting legacy of the AI boom will be *energy* infrastructure, not compute. In the user's terms, the information axis is building the energy axis's missing wood. This also explains why "picks and shovels" with long-lived assets (turbines, transformers, utilities with rate-base growth like WEC's 11% CAGR) are priced as durable compounders, while GPU-heavy neoclouds and Oracle trade like credits under stress.

These are early-stage thinning signals, not deaths yet. The equity market counter-signal is that the Big-4 hosts still sit at record index concentration (~37–39%) with CAPE ~41. The stand as a whole is priced as if no thinning will reach the canopy trees.

**Proposed measurable phase criteria** (so it's not just poetry), for a sector or technology:
| Criterion | Germination | Establishment | Vegetative | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Own revenue / capex | ≪1 | <1, rising fast | ≥1 | ≫1 (cash returned) |
| Financing | Parent reserves / equity | Parent + debt | Internal | Payouts/buybacks |
| Unit cost trend | Undefined | Steep learning curve | Learning curve + volume | Flat |
| Mortality | Few entrants | High, shakeout | Consolidation | Low; decline of old stands |
| Share of system | ~0 | <5% | 5–50% | Dominant |
